The negative is the hard half

tscircuit says where copper must stay. A laser needs to know where it must go. That is a boolean subtraction, and trace outlines self-intersect, so a polygon clipper that is subtly wrong gives you a board that looks right.

So it happens on a raster instead: paint every pad and trace into a boolean grid, grow by the kerf pullback, subtract from the board, then march the boundary back out to polygons and simplify.

One .lbrn2 → LightBurn → the B6

Every pass in one file. Open it and press go.

No Gerber, no SVG, no registration step. Holes, outline and copper were never in separate files, so there is nothing to align. Boards that come from Flux or KiCad instead take the Gerber route, where the alignment is solved by minimising the distance from every drill hole to the nearest copper: 0.270 mm on the 2025 board, against 0.853 mm for a naive bounding-box fit.

Trace width, and what 0.8 mm costs

The same board, same placement, same router. Only the trace width floor changed. A laser board has no plating and the kerf eats the edges, so wider is safer than a fab-house width — right up until it is not.

PASS0.4 mm floor — all 10 copper regions stay separate. 94.2 % cleared.
FAIL0.8 mm floor — 10 regions become 9. The tool refuses to write the job.

The failure is specific and it is not the tool being fussy. The fusion is at R2's own pads, at −3.95, −11.4 mm. A 1206 resistor leaves about a millimetre between its two pads, and a 0.8 mm trace arriving at one of them swells across that gap. Same story at any 0805 or 1206 footprint.

So 0.8 mm is right for the runs between components and wrong for the last millimetre into a small SMD pad. Through-hole parts and larger footprints have the room; chip passives do not. Routing natively at 0.8 mm rather than widening afterwards does not fix it either — the router simply narrows back to 0.47 mm where it has to squeeze, and the pads are still the pinch point.

What it checks before you cut

  • OKEvery hole inside the board outline
  • OKNo duplicate hole positions
  • OK94.7 % cleared, 5.3 % copper kept
  • OK0.05 mm pullback keeps all 3 copper regions separate

The run on the real carrier board came back differently, and correctly: no routed traces, because that routing lives in Flux, and 2 layers declared, which a single-sided process cannot make. It refused rather than producing a board of disconnected pads.

One real bug, caught during the build. The first version grew copper by 0.3 mm and called it clearance. That is backwards. The clear region is everything outside the grown copper, so every trace survives 0.3 mm wider than drawn and any two traces closer than 0.6 mm fuse into a short that looks completely deliberate on the finished board.

It is now called pullback, it defaults to 0.05 mm, and the tool counts copper boundary loops before and after and fails the run if any two merged. At 0.5 mm it correctly reports 3 loops became 2 and refuses.

Recovered, not remembered

The 2025 TRIBE board's settings survived only inside a LightBurn file nobody would think to open. Reading it back gave the five layers above, and one mapping that could not have been reconstructed from memory: every hole in the drill file has exactly one circle on the laser layer, one-to-one across all six diameters and all 59 holes, with no kerf compensation.

The 6.6 mm mounting hole runs the same 8 passes as a 0.6 mm via, which is why it is 8 and not fewer.

One thing still outstanding: run the coupon. Two parameters are genuinely missing from the 2025 file, Q-pulse width and scan line interval, because they came from device defaults and were never written down. Pulse width is the parameter that decides whether 1064 nm couples into copper at all.

The coupon is generated and waiting: three blocks of identical geometry at 200, 350 and 500 ns, five isolation moats from 0.20 to 0.50 mm, a cleared field for timing, and eighteen holes covering every drill size. Half scuffed matte, half left bright. Twenty minutes, a piece of FR4 scrap and a multimeter.

Until it runs, the recipe is an archive of what worked once, not something to replay blind.
